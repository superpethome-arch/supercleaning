Thanksgiving Cleaning Checklist: Kitchen, Guest Rooms & Bathrooms

Adriana, Super Cleaning CC· September 23, 2026·5 min read

A good Thanksgiving cleaning checklist does two things: it puts the disruptive work early and the visible work late, and it focuses on the three rooms that matter most. The kitchen, because it will be used harder than any other day of the year. The guest rooms, because someone will be sleeping in them. The bathrooms, because every guest will use one and every guest will notice. Everything else is secondary.

Kitchen

Two weeks before

  • Oven interior degreased and racks scrubbed
  • Stovetop burners and drip pans cleaned; range hood filter washed
  • Refrigerator emptied, shelves and drawers washed, expired items discarded
  • Freezer inventoried and organized to make space
  • Microwave interior cleaned
  • Dishwasher filter cleaned and a cleaning cycle run
  • Cabinet fronts and handles degreased

Two days before

  • Serving platters, gravy boat, carving set and roasting pan washed
  • Pantry cleared of items you will not use so ingredients are easy to reach
  • Trash and recycling emptied; extra bags ready

The morning of

  • Counters cleared and sanitized
  • Sink empty, dishwasher empty
  • Fresh dish towels and hand towels out

Guest Rooms

One to two weeks before

  • Windows opened to air out the room
  • Mattress vacuumed and rotated; mattress protector laundered
  • All bedding washed, including pillows if machine-washable
  • Closet cleared of storage; hangers and a luggage rack or bench provided
  • Dresser drawers emptied or at least one drawer cleared
  • Ceiling fan, light fixture, window sills and baseboards dusted
  • Floors vacuumed, including under the bed

The day before

  • Bed made with fresh linens; extra blanket folded at the foot
  • Nightstand cleared with a lamp, water glass and phone charger
  • Fresh towels set on the bed or dresser

Bathrooms

One week before

  • Grout and caulk scrubbed; mildew treated
  • Exhaust fan cover removed and cleaned
  • Shower drain cleared
  • Behind and around the toilet base cleaned
  • Medicine cabinet and vanity drawers tidied; anything personal moved out of the guest bath
  • Shower curtain and liner laundered or replaced

The day before

  • Toilet, sink, mirror and counter wiped
  • Fresh hand towels and bath towels out
  • Soap, toilet paper (with a visible spare) and a small basket of basics guests may have forgotten
  • Trash emptied

Living and Dining Areas

  • Upholstery vacuumed, cushions fluffed, throws laundered
  • Dining table cleaned, leaves inserted, chairs wiped
  • Light fixtures and ceiling fans dusted
  • Windows cleaned inside where daylight hits the table
  • Floors vacuumed and mopped; rugs cleaned
  • Entryway cleared for coats and boots

Printing This List

Most Cape Cod families split this list across two weekends and a professional visit. The two-week-out kitchen and bathroom work, the mattresses and the grout, are what a pre-holiday cleaning service handles in a single visit. The day-before items are easy to do yourself once the deep work is done.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most important room to clean before Thanksgiving?

The kitchen, followed closely by the guest bathroom. The kitchen will be used harder than any other day of the year and the guest bathroom is the room every visitor will see up close.

How early should I clean the oven before Thanksgiving?

One to two weeks before. Oven cleaning is messy and the oven needs to run once afterward to burn off any residue before you cook a turkey in it.

Should I wash pillows and comforters before guests arrive?

Yes, if they have not been washed since summer. Guest room bedding that has sat unused for months picks up dust and humidity odors, especially in Cape Cod homes near the water.
