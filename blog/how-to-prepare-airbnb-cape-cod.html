How to Prepare Your Cape Cod Airbnb for Summer Guests

Adriana — Super Cleaning CC · June 10, 2025 · 6 min read

Cape Cod's Airbnb market is one of the most competitive in New England. With thousands of listings across Falmouth, Barnstable, Chatham and the Outer Cape, the difference between a 4-star and a 5-star review often comes down to one thing: cleanliness. Here is exactly how to prepare your Cape Cod Airbnb for summer guests.

Start with a Deep Clean Before the Season Opens

Before Memorial Day weekend — the unofficial start of Cape Cod summer — every Airbnb should have a thorough spring opening clean. This means going beyond what a standard turnover covers:

  • Inside all appliances including the oven, refrigerator and microwave
  • Behind and under all furniture
  • Window sills, ceiling fans and light fixtures
  • Baseboards and door frames
  • Outdoor furniture and deck area

A fresh-smelling, deeply clean property sets the foundation for a successful season. Guests who arrive to a spotless property are far more forgiving of minor issues and far more likely to leave a positive review.

The Airbnb Turnover Checklist

Each turnover is a reset. Cape Cod Airbnb guests typically check out at 10am and check in at 3pm, leaving a 5-hour window. Here is a reliable turnover checklist:

Bedroom

  • Strip and remake all beds with fresh linens
  • Dust all surfaces including nightstands and headboards
  • Check under the bed and inside drawers
  • Vacuum and spot-clean the floor
  • Restock any amenities (extra pillows, blankets)

Bathroom

  • Scrub toilet, shower and sink thoroughly
  • Wipe mirrors and glass
  • Mop the floor
  • Replace towels with fresh ones
  • Restock soap, shampoo, toilet paper

Kitchen

  • Wipe all counters and the backsplash
  • Clean the stovetop and inside the microwave
  • Empty the dishwasher if it ran after the last guest
  • Check the refrigerator for any items left by guests
  • Wipe down cabinet fronts

What Cape Cod Guests Notice First

Research from Airbnb and short-term rental managers consistently shows guests notice three things first when they arrive:

  1. Smell. A musty or stale smell is an instant negative. Ventilate before the guests arrive if possible, and avoid heavy artificial scents.
  2. Bathroom condition. Hair in the shower drain, soap residue, and toilet cleanliness are noticed immediately.
  3. Bed quality. Wrinkled or stained linens create a poor first impression even if everything else is perfect.
